# Lucky — puzzle scratch tickets and a ticket factory

Lucky (internal ID `relic`, called Relic until 25 September 2026) is one shared scratch-ticket room for one to three people. Every ticket book is a small puzzle printed under foil, in the spirit of LinkedIn's daily games, and every ticket is bought: a careful player wins, a careless one loses. Harder difficulties open as a book gains levels, and a book's level grows with how well its tickets are played. `/expedition/<token>`, invitations, guest identities and saved desks are unchanged; `/relic` now leads to the library. The dart game, forge and bell tower of the first prototype are still refunded once at their old prices.

## Layout

Three tabs sit at the top centre beside the shared purse: **Tickets**, **Factory** and **Upgrades**. The purse keeps one width whatever it holds. Back/name and progress stay top left; sound, rules, fullscreen and Menu stay top right. Players and the invitation are also accessible from the Menu. Every tab fits one viewport. Text is kept to names, numbers and prices; rules live in How to play.

## Playing together

- **Watch** opens a little TV in a corner of the screen (drag its top bar to move it to another corner; a button makes it bigger): another player's whole screen, live. `components/game/relic-scratch-watch.tsx` renders their tab, their ticket, their factory tool and selection or the upgrade they are reading into a same-origin frame the size of _their_ screen, so a phone player is shown in their phone layout, then scales it down; their pointer is drawn in it. The page's stylesheets are copied into the frame and follow it when they change.
- **Pointers.** With a mouse on a wide screen, players on the same Factory or Upgrades tab see each other's pointers with their names, anchored to what is under them (factory floor cells, or the upgrade tree or stage); never over the tickets. On every screen, each tab carries a small dot in each other player's colour, with their initial, while they are on it.
- **Notes under the purse** for a few seconds: what another player bought (a book, an upgrade, a machine or blueprint; not belts), what their ticket just paid (in red when it paid less than it cost), and, for everyone, a book level gained.
- All of it comes over one live stream per player, `GET /api/expeditions/<token>/events?session=…` (server-sent events: `presence`, `leave`, `stroke`, `changed`). A player's tab, view and pointer go to `POST …/presence` at most every 80 ms and every 10 s as a heartbeat (`server/desk-presence.ts`, kept in memory, never saved, bounded and checked). After every command the server sends `changed`, so the others fetch the desk at once instead of at their next poll; a stroke instead carries its points, and the TV scratches its copy of that ticket with the same `rubTicket`. Purchases and payouts are kept as a short `feed` (the last 24) on the desk.

A desk link (`/expedition/<token>`) shows the desk art while it loads and, for an invited player, a small name card over it. There is no menu page of its own any more: `/relic` leads to the library, where Lucky's box opens and lists the desks.

## Tickets: the shelf and the ticket in your hand

- **Shelf** (left; a strip on portrait phones): each open book shows its painted ticket, name, the price of one ticket at the chosen level, four difficulty pips (I, II, III, IV), its numeric book level, and a gold progress bar with the book XP gathered toward the next level. Locked difficulties show a lock with the XP still missing (`xpToOpen`, `xpToInfinity`). The next locked book shows its price and is bought in order; later books are hidden entries with prices. While a ticket is being scratched the shelf waits.
- **Your hand**: there is no table. Every player holds one ticket, always open on a spotlit felt stage. Buying a ticket from the shelf puts it in your hand. The rail beside it (below on phones) shows the hearts left. The moment the ticket is over it pays by itself: the rest of the seals show what they hid, the payout counts up with its extras (Jackpot, Star, Quick, Refund), the book XP it earned beside the book's level bar, and the net win or loss, and **Again** buys the same book and level. With nothing in hand the stage shows the last book played and **Buy**.
- **Guide** (9 October 2026, `relic-ticket-guide.tsx`): every ticket, bought or blank, carries a cream slip with two short rule lines over its painted heading (beside the panel on Sea Chart; above the ticket on narrow screens), never over the cells. × hides it for that book (`localStorage` `gamehub-lucky-guide-hidden`); the book button brings it back. Ladder's card range, Gold Mine's dynamite count and Sea Chart's fleet stay shown either way.
- **Price**: a ticket costs half of what a careful player wins with it on average (`ticketPrice`: prize unit × the careful player's units with the plain ×3 jackpot × `PRICE_SHARE`). A perfect ticket roughly doubles its price; a messy one loses. Richer prizes raise prizes and prices together; the jackpot, stars, streaks, hearts, quick hands and the shop tilt the odds. When the purse cannot pay for one, Lucky Seven I is free (`houseTicket`), so a desk can never get stuck; Buy then turns into that free ticket.

## The books

Every book has four puzzle difficulties, I, II, III and **IV** (9 October 2026; first shown as ∞, then IV, renamed the same day; internal names keep `infinity`), and unlimited book levels, shared by the desk. Difficulties multiply every prize ×1, ×2, ×4 and ×5 (`LEVEL_MULT`; Mastery widens the I→II→III steps and IV stays ×1.25 of III), and bring bigger grids and fewer allowed mistakes. Level I is no longer a warm-up: every book starts with a real puzzle. A player can always pick an easier difficulty.

**Book XP.** The book level comes from book XP (`xp` in the save), and XP comes from how well each ticket went, not from how many were played. When a ticket settles it earns `round(100 × weight × quality)` XP (`ticketXp`, recorded on the ticket as `t.xp`):

- **weight** by difficulty (`XP_WEIGHTS`): I 0.1, II 0.25, III 0.5, IV 1. IV levels a book fastest; easier tickets still count, ten, four or two times more slowly.
- **quality** (`ticketQuality`): the prize units the ticket won (golden seals and payout upgrades left out; a perfect ticket's plain ×3 jackpot kept, which is the big bonus) over the careful player's average for that book and difficulty (`AVERAGES`, jackpot included). Half the average or less, which is what a losing ticket wins, earns nothing; between half and the average it rises from 0 to 1; above the average it follows the ratio up to 2 (`QUALITY_CAP`). A careful player averages about 0.7–1; a perfect ticket where the jackpot is rare (Ladder, Gold Mine, Lucky Seven III and IV) earns the full 2; a ticket that ends early on a mistake earns 0.
- One ticket never earns more than the current level's whole step, and every step is longer than the best ticket open at that level, so no ticket can skip a level.

**Levels** (`bookXpAt`, `bookExperience`): level 2 at 80 XP (8 careful I tickets), level 3 at 505 XP (17 careful II tickets more), then 1 500 XP more for level 4, 3 000 for level 5, 4 500 for level 6… (15, 30, 45… careful IV tickets, twice as many on III). Early print shortens every step (×0.7, ×0.5, ×0.3, rounded up in tickets). Every level beyond 3 adds 50% of the level 3 value to manual prizes, ticket prices and factory sales; the bonus is additive (×1.5, ×2, ×2.5…), not exponential.

**Difficulties open with the book level** (`levelOpen`, `DIFFICULTY_AT`): II at level 2, III at level 3 and IV at level 4 (`INFINITY_AT`), that is once level 3 is complete, after about 30 careful III tickets (about 15 excellent ones; Early print shortens it). Opening IV is announced to the desk once (a feed entry of kind `infinity`); every level gained is announced as before (`level`, `n` the level minus one), also when Early print raises levels at once.

The factory prints each book at its highest open difficulty up to III (`printLevel`); IV is played by hand only. Printers show the numeric book level.

Every book's tickets cost about four to six times the book before it at difficulties I–III (never less than ×2.5; a test holds this), so a later book is always the dearer and richer ticket at the same level; at IV the sizes differ more and a later book costs at least ×1.4 more. Base prices, before upgrades (I/II/III/IV): Lucky Seven 27/82/111/123, Twins 431/863/1.73K/2.7K, Garden 1.36K/4.3K/9.79K/6.62K, Ladder 7.39K/21.5K/40.1K/53.8K, Gold Mine 47.1K/91.9K/160K/165K, Sun & Moon 181K/436K/987K/2.14M, Sea Chart 562K/1.59M/2.65M/3.19M, Crown Jewels 4.22M/9.84M/22.5M/28.1M. Garden IV costs less than III because a careful player finishes it less often: prices always follow what the ticket pays on average.

IV is the same puzzle, harder: a bigger grid on every book but Crown Jewels (already 8×8, the largest a phone plays well; its IV allows a single mistake), more sevens, dynamite, hedges, signs or ships, and the jackpot comes less often (a careful player's perfect rate drops, for example Lucky Seven 35% → 13%, Garden 82% → 40%, Sea Chart 58% → 41%). `lib/games/relic/books.ts` holds the generators, `openSeal` (the rule applied when a seal comes open), `sealLocked` (Ladder rungs above the one being played, the card column, marked dynamite) and `settled` (nothing left to win). Twins, Sun & Moon and Crown Jewels are generated with solvers that prove a unique answer; Crown Jewels repairs its region map, cell by cell, until only the planted answer is left. `suggest` is a careful player that sees only what the ticket shows (it reads the unique answer of the logic books and counts cards on the Ladder); the tests, the factory averages and the prices use it.

Revealed seals show glossy prize symbols with their coin value; paid seals glow gold, golden seals pay ×10, mistakes carry a red cross, and seals shown at the end are dimmed. Foil clues (Twins numbers on their own foil colours, Garden numbers, Ladder ▲/▼, Crown colours, marked dynamite) are printed on the foil and wear away with it; hedges, signs, the Garden route and the Sea Chart counts stay above it. Locked Ladder rungs sit under a darker foil the coin cannot cut.

## Payout

`prize units × book value × book-level bonus × difficulty multiplier × 2^Richer prizes × 2^Golden touch × 1.5^book prizes × Collector`, then × the jackpot for a perfect ticket (×3, upgradable to ×20), × the star payout for a star ticket (×5 → ×12), the hot-streak bonus, +quick hands for a ticket finished within `8 s + 0.8 s per seal`, and +heart bonus for every heart left. A losing ticket simply loses; nothing is given back. A ticket is settled on the server as soon as `openSeal` ends it, inside the stroke command that caused it.

## Upgrades

Forty upgrades in six branches, drawn as little trees of medallions side by side (one branch at a time with path tabs on narrow screens). An upgrade opens once the one before it has a rank; nodes two steps away show only as a "?" silhouette. Every rank is a big step with its own price, and each upgrade's ranks are spread across the whole game (`span` in `UPGRADES`).

- **Scratching**: Bigger coin (×1.35 → ×2.8), Thin foil (72% → a touch), Quick hands (+25% → +100% for a fast ticket).
- **Luck**: Second chance (+1 mistake per rank), Star tickets (2% → 22%), Golden seal (15% → 80% chance of a ×10 seal), Bright stars (star ×5 → ×12), Hot streak.
- **Payouts**: Richer prizes (prizes and prices ×2, twelve ranks), Jackpot (×3 → ×20), Heart bonus (+5% → +35% per heart left), Golden touch (×2 everything, four ranks), Mastery (level II and III pay more), Collector (+5% per book on the shelf per rank).
- **Shop**: Bulk discount (−10% → −40%), Loyalty card (every 10th, 7th, then 5th ticket bought is free, any book; `LOYALTY`, `loyaltyDue`, counted in `punches`; it replaced Insurance on 9 October 2026, whose saved ranks were refunded), Lucky draw (5% → 25% free tickets), Early print (every book level needs 70% → 30% of the XP, so difficulties and IV open sooner).
- **Books**: each book on the shelf gets its own prize upgrade (×1.5 per rank) and a helper: Hot and cold (misses show the distance to the nearest 7), Matchmaker (pairs printed open), Garden map (more numbers), Safety rope (wrong guesses forgiven), Metal detector (dynamite marked and locked), Almanac (more printed suns and moons), Sonar (ship parts shown) and Royal decree (a crown shown).
- **Factory** (after the factory is built): Fast printers and Fast bots (×1.5 per rank), Salesmanship (factory sales ×1.4 per rank), Clever bots (bot skill 50% → 90%), Wholesale (machines −20% per rank), Bigger floor (8×5 → 16×9).

## Factory

Reworked on 8 October 2026 (see the DESIGN.md section of that date and `docs/concepts/2026-10-08-lucky-factory-rework.md`). The factory is still bought once for 1K and shared by the desk; its tickets cost nothing. Ten machines, in palette order (keys 1–0): **printer**, **belt**, **scratch bot**, **cashier** (the 1K starter), **splitter** 1.5K, **turbo** (ID `lamp`) 8K, **stamper** 60K, **gilder** 1B (around Ladder), **charm** 300B (around Gold Mine) and **bundler** 300T (around Sun & Moon). Each further copy of a machine costs 1.6–2.5× the last (belts 1.03×); Wholesale takes 20% off per rank.

- Printers print the chosen book at its highest open difficulty up to III (`printLevel`, never IV), each ticket in a third of a scratch bot's time on that book (`printTicks`), so one printer into a splitter's three exits keeps three bots busy while Fast printers and Fast bots have the same rank. Bots play a ticket at their skill and the cashier sells it at `botReward`; star tickets pay the star payout.
- Machines take tickets from any side but their front, belts from their back and sides, cashiers from every side; `intakes` and `outlets` say which. The splitter deals front, left and right in turn.
- **Boosters reach the eight cells around them** (`reach`, a 3×3 square). A turbo makes printers and bots beside it 40% faster (the ink well is gone); a charm gives printers in its glow 5% more star tickets. Both stack.
- **Stamper (×1.3) and gilder (×1.6) work on any ticket**, once each, and stack. The bundler ties three scratched tickets into one worth ×1.5 of their sum; a bundle is a new ticket, so it can be stamped and gilded too.

Editing goes through three commands. `factory-edit { remove, arrange, build }` is atomic: it removes (refunding the last price of each kind), then moves and turns the arranged machines all at once (so swaps and a group turned in place work, and machines keep what they hold), then builds (paying the running price; a printer piece may name its book). Everything is validated first; `editCost` previews `cost`, `refund` and the signed `net` without changing anything. `factory-book { cells, book }` sets every printer among the cells. `factory-blueprint` buys a machine. Because a removal refunds exactly what the last copy costs, building and removing are money-neutral, which makes undo free.

### Editing (8 October 2026)

The editor (`components/game/relic-scratch-factory.tsx`, pure rules in `relic-factory/editor.ts` with `tests/relic-factory-editor.test.mjs`) works like a drawing app. One finger or the left button acts; two fingers, the right or middle button, the wheel or a pinch (zoom) and the arrow keys move the camera.

- **Select** (V, the default): tap a machine to select it, Shift/⌘/Ctrl-click to add or remove (mouse). Drag a machine to move it, or the whole selection with it. Drag across empty floor to box-select (Shift adds); tap empty floor to clear. A single selected machine that sends tickets shows an orange **handle** on its outlet cell; dragging it pulls a belt out. Arrow keys nudge the selection one cell. With a mouse, the bar names the machine under the pointer and its state.
- **Build** (pick a machine, 1–0): a ghost shows where it goes, its ports, a booster's reach, and turns red where it cannot go or cannot be paid for. A tap places one and the machine stays in hand. It **faces the flow by itself** (`autoDir`: away from a neighbour that feeds the cell, otherwise toward one that takes from it, straight through before a corner); R or **Rotate** turns it and that choice sticks until the tool changes. Belts are drawn by dragging, each pointing along the path; walking back takes the last belt away; drawing over existing belts re-routes them; with the belt tool, a drag from a machine starts at its outlet. Q picks the machine under the pointer. Esc or a right click puts it back.
- **Erase** (E): tap or drag over machines to remove them, with the refund shown.
- **The bar** under the floor shows what is held or selected, its state in one word, every selected printer's book at once, and labelled actions: **Rotate**, **Copy**, **Delete** (+refund). A group rotates as a whole around its middle and is nudged back onto the floor.
- **Copy and paste**: Copy (the button, or ⌘C then ⌘V), ⌘D duplicates, ⌘X cuts. The copy keeps directions and books and follows the pointer as a ghost (R turns it); a click places and pays for it, and more copies can be stamped until Esc. On touch the copy appears on the nearest free spot beside the original; drag it and press **Place**.
- **Undo and redo** (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z or ⌘Y, and the two buttons in the tool rail) cover building, removing, moving, turning, pasting and book changes, 80 steps, per player. An undo another player has made impossible (they built there) fails with its error and is dropped.
- What was just built or moved stays drawn until the server confirms it, so nothing flickers back.

### The 3D floor (3 October 2026, new look 8–9 October)

Since 8 October every machine owns a colour and a silhouette, readable at the overview (about 45 px a cell on a desktop, 30 px on a phone): cobalt printer with a white paper roll, charcoal belts and splitter with yellow chevrons and arrows, an orange robot scratch bot with a coin arm, an emerald cashier under a raised gold coin sign, a violet turbo whose rotor spins up while it boosts (a low desk lamp until 9 October), a red press stamper, a white-and-gold gilder drum, a lilac charm with a floating clover and a kraft bundler with a magenta ribbon spool. The shared gold plinth is gone: each machine stands on a pad tinted with its own colour, on a pale sage tile floor with a soft grid (warm sand until 9 October). Intakes are dark mouths at the back and outlets orange-lipped chutes at the front. A turbo marks its 3×3 reach with a faint cream square and a charm with a fainter green one, both a little stronger while they help a neighbour. Model review sheets and briefs are in `work/lucky-factory-2026-10-08/`. While editing, ghosts are drawn in the machine's own colours (red where they cannot go, nearly solid until the server confirms), a held or selected booster outlines its reach, and machines are picked only up to 0.72 of a cell high so a lamp's head or the cashier's sign never hides the cells behind.

The floor is a live three.js scene (`components/game/relic-factory/`): the floor in a brass frame with chasing marquee bulbs, seen from a three-quarter camera that initially fits the whole floor into the stage. The camera sits low (46°) unless a steeper one (58°) makes the floor clearly bigger, and tall stages turn the floor a quarter, as before. Zoom from 1× to 4× using the wheel, a two-finger pinch or the +/− buttons. Pan, the third tool beside select and erase (or a right or middle-mouse drag, two fingers, or the arrow keys when nothing is selected) moves the view, and Fit restores the complete overview; keys +/−, Space (or P) and F do the same. Zoom anchors to the floor under the pointer, keeps the angle fixed and survives resizing. A pinch cancels pending building, moving and erasing, and consumes both pointer-ups. Controls have their own row above the scene so they do not cover machines. Every machine is modelled in code (`models.ts`) as an enamel toy facing +x, with a static body and a few moving parts whose poses come from what the machine is doing:

- printers spin their drum and paper roll and bring the press down as each ticket comes out, wearing the colour of the book they print and showing its ticket on the cover;
- scratch bots scrub the ticket on their platen with a coin on an arm while their head watches, rest the arm when idle and hop when a ticket comes off;
- stampers slam, gilders spin a gold brush, bundlers spin the ribbon spool and wrap the bundle;
- the till pops its drawer, turns its crank and raises a flag on each sale;
- splitters turn a red arrow to the exit they will use next;
- a turbo's rotor and a charm's clover turn slowly at rest and speed up, eased, while they boost a neighbour (the dial needle swings into the red), and a pulsing arc links them to each machine they help.

Each working machine has a **lamp**: green working, amber full (its output waits for room), red and blinking with a warning disc when it is stuck at a dead end (`status.ts`, `deadEnd` as before), dark when it has nothing to do. Printers and bots carry a gauge that fills on the frame clock. Selecting a machine, or holding one to build, shows green arrows where it takes tickets in and an orange one where it sends them. The inspector names the state in one word.

Tickets ride the belts as the books' own painted tickets, a little under half a cell long; they glide one cell per tick (linear on belts, eased in and out of machines), slide into intakes, lie on a bot's or stamper's platen while being worked, peek out of the output chute, and slide into the till. A scratched ticket carries a gold coin, a star ticket a spinning glowing star, gilded tickets turn gold, stamped ones show a red ring, and bundles are three tied with a ribbon. A belt fed only from one side curves round to its front, and its tickets ride the middle of the curve; a belt with a stuck ticket stops scrolling. Each sale throws gold coins and sparks from the till, a coin arcs from the till to the purse (which bumps as it lands), and the amount rises over the till; sales close together on one till share a label and at most eight coins fly at once (`fx.ts`). Reduced motion keeps one coin burst and the label, without flying coins.

Draw calls stay bounded as the factory grows: each machine kind is one instanced mesh per material plus one per moving part, the three belt shapes are instanced with their tops scrolled in the shader, tickets are one instanced mesh per book, and gauges, halos, sparks and badges are camera-facing instanced quads. Full model geometry remains at every zoom. The main scene always uses antialiasing, full-size ticket textures with mipmaps and anisotropic filtering, and 2048 shadows (4096 when inspecting closely). Arena's frame pacer (`components/game/arena/pacer.ts`) follows a factory-specific ladder (`quality.ts`): reduce shadow refreshes first, then resolution by at most 15%, never below one drawing pixel per CSS pixel. The Watch TV also uses antialiasing and one drawing pixel per CSS pixel, with small ticket textures and no shadows at about 30 fps. Palette, inspector and notification pictures are rendered once from the same models (`icons.ts`), so they always match the floor. Pointers on the floor are anchored to floor cells through the camera (`anchor.ts`).

The browser runs the factory step on each tick boundary and carries every server snapshot forward by the active time it has already shown, so the floor never steps back. Ticks stay 500 ms and run on active time only; see `lib/games/relic/factory.ts`. The painted v2 sprites are no longer drawn; their sources stay in `docs/concepts/2026-09-26-lucky-factory-intakes/`.

## Pacing

With IV and book XP (9 October 2026) the same careful run reaches Crown Jewels after about 2 hours 37 minutes; IV pays ×5 rather than ×8 so that the run stays above the two and a half hour mark (×8 brought it to about 2h20).

The pacing test plays a careful player who buys the newest book they can afford (three seconds per ticket plus a second or two per seal, faster with coin and foil upgrades), simple straight factory lines and the cheapest upgrade. With uncapped levels and the harder Twins start it reaches Crown Jewels in about 2 hours 40 minutes (27 September 2026); the test keeps the factory within four minutes, Twins within ten, Ladder after fifteen, Gold Mine after thirty-five and Crown Jewels after two and a half hours. People will be slower, and sloppy play loses money on tickets.

## Saves

Version 5 (9 October 2026) stores book XP. Version 4 and older desks counted tickets (`books`, and difficulty III tickets in `top`); each book gets the XP that puts it at the same book level with the same fraction of the way to the next one, so books already past level 3 have IV open at once. `top` is dropped; `books` stays as a count of finished tickets. Version 3 desks keep everything except the free tickets that lay on the tables, which are retired; every player starts with an empty hand. Version 1 and 2 desks keep their unlocked books and ticket counts, mapped by shelf position (Pocket Change → Lucky Seven, Silver Ribbons → Twins, Secret Garden → Garden, Twin Moons → Ladder, Copper Grain → Gold Mine, Lantern → Sun & Moon, Golden Atlas → Sea Chart, Crown Jewels → Crown Jewels). Every old upgrade rank is refunded at its old price except the factory floor, which keeps its size. The factory keeps its machines; printers and tickets on belts are mapped to the new books. Unfinished old tickets are retired.

The factory has its own version (2, 8 October 2026), upgraded once on load by `migrateFactory`: ink wells become lamps where they stand, the ink blueprint becomes the lamp blueprint (refunded at 20K when the lamp was already owned), unknown machines are dropped and tickets keep their marks. Machines keep their cells and directions; layouts that relied on the old rules (stamper on blank tickets only, side-only boosters) keep working, and usually work better.

## Artwork

Each book is a differently shaped painted ticket with its own print style: a 1950s fairground ticket with a tear-off stub (Lucky Seven), twin art deco arches (Twins), a scalloped Victorian seed packet (Garden), a tall Swiss-poster tower (Ladder), a riveted octagonal mine sign (Gold Mine), a round celestial medallion (Sun & Moon), a torn parchment chart (Sea Chart) and a jewelled heraldic shield (Crown Jewels). The prize symbols are one generated 4×4 sheet. The square cover and the 3:1 lid carry the exact title "Lucky". Prompts, logs and choices are in `docs/concepts/2026-09-25-lucky-tickets/`; source PNGs and the rejected variants are in the art archive beside the repo. `scripts/optimize-lucky-art.mjs` trims each ticket, measures its empty play panel by flood fill into `components/game/relic-scratch-art.ts`, rebuilds the symbol sheet with clear gutters and writes the covers. The desk background plates are unchanged.

## Persistence and authority

The browser predicts foil erasure and the book rules for immediate response; the server repeats the same bitmap calculation and `openSeal` from bounded stroke points (up to `STROKE_POINTS`, 300, at four decimals; `breaks` mark where the coin was lifted). The browser drops pointer samples closer than 0.4 of the coin to the last one and merges everything queued while a command is in flight, so at most one stroke command is ever waiting. `rubTicket` skips seals beyond the coin's reach, with the same result. Commands name the ticket and the stroke sequence; retries are idempotent through request IDs; no client submits a payout. SQLite transactions serialize shared purchases.
