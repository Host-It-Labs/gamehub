# Lucky — puzzle scratch tickets and a ticket factory

Lucky (internal ID `relic`, called Relic until 25 September 2026) is one shared scratch-ticket room for one to three people. Every ticket book is a small puzzle printed under foil, in the spirit of LinkedIn's daily games, and every ticket is bought: a careful player wins, a careless one loses. Harder levels open once a book has been played. `/expedition/<token>`, invitations, guest identities and saved desks are unchanged; `/relic` now leads to the library. The dart game, forge and bell tower of the first prototype are still refunded once at their old prices.

## Layout

Three tabs sit at the top centre beside the shared purse: **Tickets**, **Factory** and **Upgrades**. The purse keeps one width whatever it holds. Back/name and progress stay top left; sound, rules, fullscreen and Menu stay top right. Players and the invitation are also accessible from the Menu. Every tab fits one viewport. Text is kept to names, numbers and prices; rules live in How to play.

## Playing together

- **Watch** opens a little TV in a corner of the screen (drag its top bar to move it to another corner; a button makes it bigger): another player's whole screen, live. `components/game/relic-scratch-watch.tsx` renders their tab, their ticket, their factory tool and selection or the upgrade they are reading into a same-origin frame the size of *their* screen, so a phone player is shown in their phone layout, then scales it down; their pointer is drawn in it. The page's stylesheets are copied into the frame and follow it when they change.
- **Pointers.** With a mouse on a wide screen, players on the same Factory or Upgrades tab see each other's pointers with their names, anchored to what is under them (factory floor cells, or the upgrade tree or stage); never over the tickets. On every screen, each tab carries a small dot in each other player's colour, with their initial, while they are on it.
- **Notes under the purse** for a few seconds: what another player bought (a book, an upgrade, a machine or blueprint; not belts), what their ticket just paid (in red when it paid less than it cost), and, for everyone, a book level gained.
- All of it comes over one live stream per player, `GET /api/expeditions/<token>/events?session=…` (server-sent events: `presence`, `leave`, `stroke`, `changed`). A player's tab, view and pointer go to `POST …/presence` at most every 80 ms and every 10 s as a heartbeat (`server/desk-presence.ts`, kept in memory, never saved, bounded and checked). After every command the server sends `changed`, so the others fetch the desk at once instead of at their next poll; a stroke instead carries its points, and the TV scratches its copy of that ticket with the same `rubTicket`. Purchases and payouts are kept as a short `feed` (the last 24) on the desk.

A desk link (`/expedition/<token>`) shows the desk art while it loads and, for an invited player, a small name card over it. There is no menu page of its own any more: `/relic` leads to the library, where Lucky's box opens and lists the desks.

## Tickets: the shelf and the ticket in your hand

- **Shelf** (left; a strip on portrait phones): each open book shows its painted ticket, name, the price of one ticket at the chosen level, three difficulty pips (I, II, III), its numeric book level, and a gold progress bar with the exact completed/required ticket count. Locked levels show a lock and say how many more tickets open them. The next locked book shows its price and is bought in order; later books are hidden entries with prices. While a ticket is being scratched the shelf waits.
- **Your hand**: there is no table. Every player holds one ticket, always open on a spotlit felt stage. Buying a ticket from the shelf puts it in your hand. The rail beside it (below on phones) shows the hearts left. The moment the ticket is over it pays by itself: the rest of the seals show what they hid, the payout counts up with its extras (Jackpot, Star, Quick, Refund) and the net win or loss, and **Again** buys the same book and level. With nothing in hand the stage shows the last book played and **Buy**.
- **Price**: a ticket costs half of what a careful player wins with it on average (`ticketPrice`: prize unit × the careful player's units with the plain ×3 jackpot × `PRICE_SHARE`). A perfect ticket roughly doubles its price; a messy one loses. Richer prizes raise prizes and prices together; the jackpot, stars, streaks, hearts, quick hands and the shop tilt the odds. When the purse cannot pay for one, Lucky Seven I is free (`houseTicket`), so a desk can never get stuck.

## The books

Every book has three puzzle difficulties and unlimited book levels. A level opens after 8 and 25 finished tickets of that book, shared by the desk (sooner with Early print); a player can always pick an easier level. Levels multiply every prize ×1, ×2 and ×4 (more with Mastery), and bring bigger grids and fewer allowed mistakes. Level I is no longer a warm-up: every book starts with a real puzzle.

The factory prints each book at its highest open difficulty. Book levels 1–3 follow those unlocks. After III opens, hard tickets raise the book to level 4 after 15, level 5 after another 30, level 6 after another 45, and so on. Early print reduces the 15-ticket step (rounded up). Every level beyond 3 adds 50% of the level 3 value to manual prizes, ticket prices and factory sales; the bonus is additive (×1.5, ×2, ×2.5…), not exponential. Existing `books` and `top` counters carry straight over, including old maxed books (now level 4 or higher). Only difficulty III tickets count after book level 3; the easier difficulties remain playable. The shelf always shows progress to the next level and printers show the numeric book level.

Every book's tickets cost about four to six times the book before it at every level (never less than ×2.5; a test holds this), so a later book is always the dearer and richer ticket at the same level. Base prices, before upgrades: Lucky Seven 27/82/111, Twins 431/863/1.73K, Garden 1.36K/4.3K/9.79K, Ladder 7.39K/21.5K/40.1K, Gold Mine 47.1K/91.9K/160K, Sun & Moon 181K/436K/987K, Sea Chart 562K/1.59M/2.65M, Crown Jewels 4.22M/9.84M/22.5M.

`lib/games/relic/books.ts` holds the generators, `openSeal` (the rule applied when a seal comes open), `sealLocked` (Ladder rungs above the one being played, the card column, marked dynamite) and `settled` (nothing left to win). Twins, Sun & Moon and Crown Jewels are generated with solvers that prove a unique answer; Crown Jewels repairs its region map, cell by cell, until only the planted answer is left. `suggest` is a careful player that sees only what the ticket shows (it reads the unique answer of the logic books and counts cards on the Ladder); the tests, the factory averages and the prices use it.

Revealed seals show glossy prize symbols with their coin value; paid seals glow gold, golden seals pay ×10, mistakes carry a red cross, and seals shown at the end are dimmed. Foil clues (Twins numbers on their own foil colours, Garden numbers, Ladder ▲/▼, Crown colours, marked dynamite) are printed on the foil and wear away with it; hedges, signs, the Garden route and the Sea Chart counts stay above it. Locked Ladder rungs sit under a darker foil the coin cannot cut.

## Payout

`prize units × book value × book-level bonus × difficulty multiplier × 2^Richer prizes × 2^Golden touch × 1.5^book prizes × Collector`, then × the jackpot for a perfect ticket (×3, upgradable to ×20), × the star payout for a star ticket (×5 → ×12), the hot-streak bonus, +quick hands for a ticket finished within `8 s + 0.8 s per seal`, and +heart bonus for every heart left. Insurance returns part of what a ticket lost. A ticket is settled on the server as soon as `openSeal` ends it, inside the stroke command that caused it.

## Upgrades

Forty upgrades in six branches, drawn as little trees of medallions side by side (one branch at a time with path tabs on narrow screens). An upgrade opens once the one before it has a rank; nodes two steps away show only as a "?" silhouette. Every rank is a big step with its own price, and each upgrade's ranks are spread across the whole game (`span` in `UPGRADES`).

- **Scratching**: Bigger coin (×1.35 → ×2.8), Thin foil (72% → a touch), Quick hands (+25% → +100% for a fast ticket).
- **Luck**: Second chance (+1 mistake per rank), Star tickets (2% → 22%), Golden seal (15% → 80% chance of a ×10 seal), Bright stars (star ×5 → ×12), Hot streak.
- **Payouts**: Richer prizes (prizes and prices ×2, twelve ranks), Jackpot (×3 → ×20), Heart bonus (+5% → +35% per heart left), Golden touch (×2 everything, four ranks), Mastery (level II and III pay more), Collector (+5% per book on the shelf per rank).
- **Shop**: Bulk discount (−10% → −40%), Insurance (25% → 75% of a loss back), Lucky draw (5% → 25% free tickets), Early print (levels open sooner).
- **Books**: each book on the shelf gets its own prize upgrade (×1.5 per rank) and a helper: Hot and cold (misses show the distance to the nearest 7), Matchmaker (pairs printed open), Garden map (more numbers), Safety rope (wrong guesses forgiven), Metal detector (dynamite marked and locked), Almanac (more printed suns and moons), Sonar (ship parts shown) and Royal decree (a crown shown).
- **Factory** (after the factory is built): Fast printers and Fast bots (×1.5 per rank), Salesmanship (factory sales ×1.4 per rank), Clever bots (bot skill 50% → 90%), Wholesale (machines −20% per rank), Bigger floor (8×5 → 16×9).

## Factory

The factory is still bought once for 1K and shared by the desk; its tickets cost nothing. The other blueprints cost 1.5K (splitter), 8K (lamp), 20K (ink well), 60K (stamper), 1B (gilder, around Ladder), 300B (charm, around Gold Mine) and 300T (bundler, around Sun & Moon), so the factory gains a machine with each era instead of finishing in the first half hour; each further copy of a machine costs 1.6–2.5× the last (belts 1.03×). It is drawn in 3D (see below). Printers print the chosen book at its highest open level and show it, each ticket in a third of a scratch bot's time on that book (`printTicks`), so on every book one printer into a splitter's three exits keeps three bots busy while Fast printers and Fast bots have the same rank; Fast printers or an ink well let it feed more; scratch bots play a ticket at their skill and the cashier sells it at `botReward` (the careful-player average from `AVERAGES`, × bot skill, with the jackpot weighted by the perfect rate). Star tickets pay the star payout. **A placed machine can be dragged to any empty cell** (`factory-move`), keeping what it holds; a tap selects it to turn, remove or change it. With a mouse, dragging over empty floor or Shift/⌘-clicking selects several machines, which then move (`factory-shift`), turn (`factory-rotate` with `cells`) and are removed together; Ctrl/⌘-A selects them all.

The palette starts on the select tool; picking a machine holds it (R turns the machine being placed) and picking it again puts it down. A locked machine is explained in the bar below the floor and bought with **Unlock**, which then holds it. Keys: V select, E remove, R rotate, 1–0 the machines in palette order, Delete removes the selection, Esc lets go of everything; with a mouse the keys are shown on the buttons.

### The 3D floor (3 October 2026)

The floor is a live three.js scene (`components/game/relic-factory/`): the painted floor tile in a brass frame with chasing marquee bulbs, seen from a three-quarter camera that initially fits the whole floor into the stage. The camera sits low (46°) unless a steeper one (58°) makes the floor clearly bigger, and tall stages turn the floor a quarter, as before. Zoom from 1× to 4× using the wheel, a two-finger pinch or the +/− buttons. The pan button (or a middle-mouse drag) moves the view, and Fit restores the complete overview; keys +/−, P and F do the same. Zoom anchors to the floor under the pointer, keeps the angle fixed and survives resizing. A pinch cancels pending building, moving and erasing, and consumes both pointer-ups. Controls have their own row above the scene so they do not cover machines. Every machine is modelled in code (`models.ts`) as an enamel-and-brass toy facing +x, with a static body and a few moving parts whose poses come from what the machine is doing:

- printers spin their drum and paper roll and bring the press down as each ticket comes out, wearing the colour of the book they print and showing its ticket on the cover;
- scratch bots scrub the ticket on their platen with a coin on an arm while their head watches, rest the arm when idle and hop when a ticket comes off;
- stampers slam, gilders spin a gold brush, bundlers spin the ribbon spool and wrap the bundle;
- the till pops its drawer, turns its crank and raises a flag on each sale;
- splitters turn a red arrow to the exit they will use next;
- lamps, ink wells and charms glow, bubble or spin only while they boost a neighbour, and a pulsing arc links them to each machine they help.

Each working machine has a **lamp**: green working, amber full (its output waits for room), red and blinking with a warning disc when it is stuck at a dead end (`status.ts`, `deadEnd` as before), dark when it has nothing to do. Printers and bots carry a gauge that fills on the frame clock. Selecting a machine, or holding one to build, shows green arrows where it takes tickets in and an orange one where it sends them. The inspector names the state in one word.

Tickets ride the belts as the books' own painted tickets, a little under half a cell long; they glide one cell per tick (linear on belts, eased in and out of machines), slide into intakes, lie on a bot's or stamper's platen while being worked, peek out of the output chute, and slide into the till. A scratched ticket carries a gold coin, a star ticket a spinning glowing star, gilded tickets turn gold, stamped ones show a red ring, and bundles are three tied with a ribbon. A belt fed only from one side curves round to its front, and its tickets ride the middle of the curve; a belt with a stuck ticket stops scrolling. Each sale throws gold coins and sparks from the till, a coin arcs from the till to the purse (which bumps as it lands), and the amount rises over the till; sales close together on one till share a label and at most eight coins fly at once (`fx.ts`). Reduced motion keeps one coin burst and the label, without flying coins.

Draw calls stay bounded as the factory grows: each machine kind is one instanced mesh per material plus one per moving part, the three belt shapes are instanced with their tops scrolled in the shader, tickets are one instanced mesh per book, and gauges, halos, sparks and badges are camera-facing instanced quads. Full model geometry remains at every zoom. The main scene always uses antialiasing, full-size ticket textures with mipmaps and anisotropic filtering, and 2048 shadows (4096 when inspecting closely). Arena's frame pacer (`components/game/arena/pacer.ts`) follows a factory-specific ladder (`quality.ts`): reduce shadow refreshes first, then resolution by at most 15%, never below one drawing pixel per CSS pixel. The Watch TV also uses antialiasing and one drawing pixel per CSS pixel, with small ticket textures and no shadows at about 30 fps. Palette, inspector and notification pictures are rendered once from the same models (`icons.ts`), so they always match the floor. Pointers on the floor are anchored to floor cells through the camera (`anchor.ts`).

The browser runs the factory step on each tick boundary and carries every server snapshot forward by the active time it has already shown, so the floor never steps back. Everything else (belts, splitters, lamps, ink wells, stampers, gilders, charms, bundlers, 500 ms ticks, active time only) is unchanged; see `lib/games/relic/factory.ts`. The painted v2 sprites are no longer drawn; their sources stay in `docs/concepts/2026-09-26-lucky-factory-intakes/`.

## Pacing

The pacing test plays a careful player who buys the newest book they can afford (three seconds per ticket plus a second or two per seal, faster with coin and foil upgrades), simple straight factory lines and the cheapest upgrade. With uncapped levels and the harder Twins start it reaches Crown Jewels in about 2 hours 40 minutes (27 September 2026); the test keeps the factory within four minutes, Twins within ten, Ladder after fifteen, Gold Mine after thirty-five and Crown Jewels after two and a half hours. People will be slower, and sloppy play loses money on tickets.

## Development workshop

In development only, Lucky → Play → **Dev workshop** creates a separate saved desk, available from either the library or a table. All eight books are at levels 12–19 with all difficulties open, 500Qa coins, most upgrades one rank short of full, a full 16×9 floor and every blueprint. Its 68 pieces include eight working mixed-book lines, buffers filled by an actual minute of simulation, faster printers than bots, imperfect booster placement and an unfinished side line. It is deliberately busy and inefficient. Continue it solo or bring it into a table using the usual saved-desk picker; no existing save is overwritten. The server rejects the preset outside `NODE_ENV=development`.

## Saves

Version 4. Version 3 desks keep everything except the free tickets that lay on the tables, which are retired; every player starts with an empty hand. Version 1 and 2 desks keep their unlocked books and ticket counts, mapped by shelf position (Pocket Change → Lucky Seven, Silver Ribbons → Twins, Secret Garden → Garden, Twin Moons → Ladder, Copper Grain → Gold Mine, Lantern → Sun & Moon, Golden Atlas → Sea Chart, Crown Jewels → Crown Jewels). Every old upgrade rank is refunded at its old price except the factory floor, which keeps its size. The factory keeps its machines; printers and tickets on belts are mapped to the new books. Unfinished old tickets are retired.

## Artwork

Each book is a differently shaped painted ticket with its own print style: a 1950s fairground ticket with a tear-off stub (Lucky Seven), twin art deco arches (Twins), a scalloped Victorian seed packet (Garden), a tall Swiss-poster tower (Ladder), a riveted octagonal mine sign (Gold Mine), a round celestial medallion (Sun & Moon), a torn parchment chart (Sea Chart) and a jewelled heraldic shield (Crown Jewels). The prize symbols are one generated 4×4 sheet. The square cover and the 3:1 lid carry the exact title "Lucky". Prompts, logs and choices are in `docs/concepts/2026-09-25-lucky-tickets/`; source PNGs and the rejected variants are in the art archive beside the repo. `scripts/optimize-lucky-art.mjs` trims each ticket, measures its empty play panel by flood fill into `components/game/relic-scratch-art.ts`, rebuilds the symbol sheet with clear gutters and writes the covers. The desk background plates are unchanged.

## Persistence and authority

The browser predicts foil erasure and the book rules for immediate response; the server repeats the same bitmap calculation and `openSeal` from bounded stroke points (up to `STROKE_POINTS`, 300, at four decimals; `breaks` mark where the coin was lifted). The browser drops pointer samples closer than 0.4 of the coin to the last one and merges everything queued while a command is in flight, so at most one stroke command is ever waiting. `rubTicket` skips seals beyond the coin's reach, with the same result. Commands name the ticket and the stroke sequence; retries are idempotent through request IDs; no client submits a payout. SQLite transactions serialize shared purchases.

## Reference and validation

The user-provided [Scritchy Scratchy Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3948120/Scritchy_Scratchy/) informed the tactile scratch/reveal/upgrade loop, LinkedIn's daily games (Zip, Tango, Queens) the book puzzles, and the request for big, game-changing upgrades the upgrade ladders. There is no real-money gambling.

`tests/relic-scratch.test.mjs` covers stroke geometry (including skipped seals and merged strokes against the plain rule), automatic payout, ticket prices and the house ticket, one ticket per hand, insurance and lucky draws, levels, every book's generator and rule (including unique answers for Twins, Sun & Moon and Crown Jewels) and helpers, golden seals, the factory averages against the careful player, book prices and upgrade ranks, version 1, 2 and 3 migration, the factory and moving machines, per-member server hands, retries, active time, uncapped book levels and their scaling, dev-save isolation, the feed, group moves, the price steps between books and the pacing run. `tests/relic-presence.test.mjs` covers the live stream: presence relayed, checked and bounded, and commands and strokes relayed once. `scripts/relic-smoke.mjs` is the production smoke. `scripts/relic-browser-qa.mjs` still targets an older interface.

## Table continuity (26 September 2026)

The library and table share one setup dialog with New desk and Continue. Continuing attaches the same saved desk, preserving coins, tickets, books, factory and upgrades. All existing members plus the table must fit three seats; a failed attachment adds nobody. Menu → Table settings stays connected to the originating table so its creator can bring everyone back to the lobby or close the table without deleting the desk.
