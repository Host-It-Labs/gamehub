## Direction

- **Cowboy:** a seasoned frontier gunslinger, with an authored adult face,
  credible hands and connected shoulders/elbows. Heavy sand canvas, felt,
  worked leather, walnut and brass have distinct surfaces. His lever rifle
  remains held in both hands and clears the torso through its complete motion.
- **Samurai:** a focused adult warrior with a readable face, substantial
  shoulders and planted legs. Cobalt lacquer plates, silk cords, folded
  hakama, metal blade and straw sandals retain distinct construction. Both
  hands remain attached to the katana through anticipation, cut and recovery.
- **Wizard:** a weathered travelling caster with shaped facial planes,
  separated silver hair/beard locks and convincing open fingers. Weighty
  woven robes, a charcoal mantle, felt hat and knotted wood staff carry the
  character. Boot steps and casting weight transfer stay legible.

Existing costume detail is useful. More decoration cannot replace convincing
primary anatomy, material contrast or continuous joints. Avoid inflated limbs,
uniform plastic surfaces and repeated grain. Match the mature stylized
collector world and the existing painted action-card language.

## Review

Each source needs clay front, side, rear and three-quarter views, anatomy and
material close-ups, intended-camera views and representative extreme poses.
Root review records concrete visual observations before runtime reduction.
Compare source and game copies at identical cameras and motion. Inspect idle,
walk/turn, attack/recovery, deployment, slower/faster states and building
contact. Check the actual game at phone, tablet and desktop sizes. Report
browser checks separately from unavailable physical touch or mobile GPU tests.

The current scene inspection shows useful costumes and silhouettes, but simple
rounded facial structure, mitten-like hands and inflated sleeve transitions.
The new sources must resolve these structural weaknesses. Automated tests
alone do not establish visual quality or user acceptance.
