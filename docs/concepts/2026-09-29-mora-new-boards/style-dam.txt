STYLE. A lovingly handmade layered cut-paper diorama with real depth, photographed from a high oblique overhead angle. Every object is cut, folded or stacked from thick fibrous cardstock and kraft paper: visible paper grain, white cut edges, folded creases, stacked layers with a soft cast shadow under each, levels joined by little paper steps and ramps. Shapes are simplified and graphic like real paper craft. This is an old hydroelectric dam high in the mountains, with its turbine hall, a cable car station and a small summit hut, abandoned long ago and taken back by the mountain: layered white and pale blue paper snowfields and snow drifts on every ledge, glacier ice of pale cyan card, grey granite boulders and scree of stacked paper, weathered pale concrete walls streaked with ochre and sage lichen, paper icicles, rusted iron railings and one faded red cable car cabin. Mountain nature has grown over the ruins: dark green dwarf pines and golden larches of cut card, white edelweiss and deep blue gentian flowers in small alpine meadows, cushions of pink moss campion between the stones. No leafy forest, no palm trees, no ivy, no ferns, no desert plants. Palette of snow white, ice blue, slate grey and pine green, with warm accents of larch gold, faded red and gentian blue. Crisp bright daylight from the upper left with long soft blue shadows falling toward the lower right. It must read clearly as a real paper model: matte, flat colour fields, crisp paper edges. Nothing photorealistic, nothing painterly, nothing glossy, plastic, clay or smooth digital 3D.
