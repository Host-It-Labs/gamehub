STYLE. A lovingly handmade layered cut-paper diorama with real depth, photographed from a high oblique overhead angle. Every object is cut, folded or stacked from thick fibrous cardstock and kraft paper: visible paper grain, white cut edges, folded creases, stacked layers with a soft cast shadow under each, levels joined by little paper steps and ramps. Shapes are simplified and graphic like real paper craft. This is an old roadside desert motel with its diner and gas station, abandoned long ago and taken back by the desert: drifts of layered sand-coloured paper dunes spilling through doorways, red and ochre sandstone boulders and small mesas of stacked paper, cracked pale grey asphalt, sun-faded turquoise and coral paint on stucco walls, rusted metal signs and one rusted old car. The desert has overgrown the ruins the way a desert does, not like a forest: tall saguaro and round prickly pear cacti of cut green card, pink and yellow cactus blossoms, orange desert marigolds, grey-green sagebrush tufts, tumbleweeds, dry golden grass and bleached branches. No forest, no leafy trees, no palm trees, no ivy, no ferns, no moss, no water. Palette of warm sand, terracotta, dusty rose, ochre, sage and cactus green, with small accents of faded turquoise and rust. Warm late-afternoon sunlight from the upper left with long soft shadows falling toward the lower right. It must read clearly as a real paper model: matte, flat colour fields, crisp paper edges. Nothing photorealistic, nothing painterly, nothing glossy, plastic, clay or smooth digital 3D.
