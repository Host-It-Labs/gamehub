# Rota covers about roads and trains, 10 October 2026

William found the 9 October covers (the model-railway mill town panorama)
generic and too zoomed out, and the "ROTA" lettering generic too. This run
regenerates both Rota covers (the square library cover and the 3:1 setup-lid
`wideCover`) about what you do in Rota: laying streets and railway track
square by square, a train mid-route, a road being extended, junctions and a
goods station, framed close with few big readable elements. The title,
"Rota" (capital R, lowercase o-t-a), is lettered in cream enamel with a
railway line running down every stroke, the o a loop of track, the t's
crossbar a street at a level crossing, the a's tail a cobbled street, and a
small train riding the word. The mill town's identity is kept (cel-shaded
toon tones with ink outlines, brick, slate, cobbles, railway liveries in deep
green, maroon and cream, golden hour into dusk, top-and-tail goods trains).

## Generation

Text-only generations, one image per Codex invocation, two variants (`-a`,
`-b`) per image, all four launched at once through `batch.sh` → `gen.sh`
(copied from the 9 October run: `gen.sh` requires exactly one PNG in the
run's own `~/.codex/generated_images/<session id>/` folder and checks the
saved copy by sha256). No reference images, no edits. Model `gpt-6-luna`
(newest Luna from `.claude/skills/artwork/newest-luna.mjs`), low reasoning
effort, codex-cli 0.161.0, stdin closed. Logs are in `logs/`, PNGs in
`sources/`. Both variants of an image share the prompt file in `prompts/`
(exact text below).

What changed versus the 9 October prompts, as instructions: a close
three-quarter view of about four by four plots instead of a bird's-eye town
panorama; the subjects are the act of building (a hovering street piece
lining up with the built street, dashed ink guide lines on the next plots, a
junction, a goods train mid-route, a brick road bridge, a goods shed); the
title is "Rota" in mixed case made of track and street instead of "ROTA" in
plain railway-poster capitals; a "not like" clause names the other games'
materials.
