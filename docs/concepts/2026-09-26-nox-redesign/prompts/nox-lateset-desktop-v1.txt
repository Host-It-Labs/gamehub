Use case: ui-mockup. Create one complete in-game screen of a desktop browser card game, concept art for a new art direction of a trick-taking game called Nox, played at night. Landscape 1536x1024 PNG, largest native landscape size, highest quality, no watermark.

Style, direction LATE SET: confident mid-century modernist flat graphic design, like a bold 1950s or 1960s jazz record sleeve, all big blocks of colour, circles and rhythmic stripes. Everything is built from a small number of large, clean, flat shapes with crisp edges: rectangles, circles, half circles, triangles and long parallel stripes, arranged on a clear grid with generous calm areas. Flat colour only: no brush texture, no paper grain, no ink texture, no painterly shading, no soft gradients, no fine detail. Depth comes only from overlapping flat shapes, flat offset shadows and one translucent flat shape of light. It looks designed, deliberate, witty and calm, like a real printed poster by a great graphic designer, never like a digital painting. Palette, strictly limited: black and a warm charcoal, deep cobalt blue, warm cream, tangerine orange as the accent, and a little mustard.

World: a card game played on the stage of a small jazz club after the last set, seen straight from above like a flat stage plan, the instruments left resting where the band put them down. The play surface is the stage floor: one large rectangle of deep cobalt blue, with a slim cream line painted around it a little inside its edge; the rim band outside that line is a calm, continuous edge of black stage boards on all four sides. Around the stage, filling the picture to every edge: at the top, a drum kit seen from above as a cluster of flat circles, cream drum heads and mustard cymbals with thin concentric rings; at the upper right, a grand piano seen from above as a big black curved shape with its lid open, showing long thin mustard strings and a stripe of cream and black keys; at the left, an upright double bass lying on its side, a flat tangerine hourglass shape with a long black neck; at the lower right, a trumpet resting on the floor as a flat mustard shape; a coiled cable drawn as a cream spiral; the club floor around in charcoal with a bold pattern of large black and charcoal squares; a few scattered sheets of music drawn as plain cream rectangles with thin horizontal lines only. Light: one spotlight above the upper left corner is the single light source; its light is one large translucent cream circle falling across the upper left of the stage, and every flat shadow falls toward the lower right.

Layout of the game screen, drawn in this style. The play area is a wide rectangle, face-on and perfectly flat in true plan view, spanning about the middle 72 percent of the image width and running from about 13 percent to about 70 percent of the image height; the scenery surrounds it on every side and fills the picture to every edge. Around the play area runs a calm, continuous rim band, empty so that name tags can sit on it anywhere. Four players sit around the table, each shown only as a compact horizontal pill-shaped seat tag lying on the rim band: a small circular avatar made of a simple flat geometric pattern (no faces), a short name and a small score number. The viewer's tag lies on the bottom rim, clearly left of centre, about one third of the way along from the left end; the others lie on the middle of the left rim, the middle of the top rim and the middle of the right rim. The top rim tag has a bright ring in the accent colour around its avatar, showing that it is that player's turn. Just inside the rim, four small flat chevron arrows, one at the middle of each side, chase each other clockwise around the table: the one on the top side points right, the one on the right side points down, the one on the bottom side points left and the one on the left side points up. In the middle of the play area lies the current trick: three face-up cards lying flat and straight, slightly apart, each one nudged toward the player who played it (left, top and right), each with a flat offset shadow that falls away from the light. No chairs, no seats, no benches, no people and no hands anywhere.

Cards: crisp cream-white cards with small rounded corners and a thin inner border, each with one large bold numeral at the top left in a heavy modernist sans serif and one large flat geometric suit shape in the centre. Five suits, each with its own colour: spades black, hearts tangerine orange, clubs deep cobalt blue, diamonds mustard, and a fifth suit, Storm, whose card face is split diagonally into tangerine and black with a bold cream lightning bolt. The storm cards are the dangerous ones.

The hand dock at the bottom, below the play area: a long floating tray with rounded ends in flat black with a thin cream edge, like a strip of stage board, about 60 percent of the image width, resting just above the bottom edge, holding ten overlapping face-up cards in one straight row, each overlapping the next so that its numeral and suit stay visible, one of them a storm card; one card is lifted clear above the others and brought to the front, selected. At the left end of the tray: one flat die drawn as a cream rounded square showing a heart suit shape, and beside it two small round flat tokens, one with a shield shape and the number 2, one with a four-pronged grappling-hook shape. At the right end of the tray: a flat rounded rectangular button in the accent colour with the single word Play, and next to it a small round cream button with a curved undo arrow.

Navigation drawn on top, and nothing else: top-left corner, a small round cream button with a left arrow, and beside it the word Nox in a bold modernist sans serif. Top-right corner, one small round cream menu button with three short horizontal lines. Top centre, one small cream pill showing 2/4. No other interface, no status bar, no captions, no helper text.

Text: the only words in the image are Nox and Play. The only other characters are the card numerals from 1 to 10, the round count 2/4, the small score numbers and the four short names on the seat tags: You, Rook, Static and Moth. No other letters, words, labels, logos, brand names or watermark anywhere, not on the instruments, the sheet music or the floor.

Not like: not a painted or rendered fantasy scene; no pirates, no treasure chest, no gold coins, no skulls, no candles, no dramatic glow and no clutter. Not a harbour and no boats. Not cut paper or kraft card. Not woodcut, linocut, risograph or any ink printmaking texture. Not cel-shaded outlined cartoon or anime. Not a 3D render. Not a stock vector illustration with gradients. No musicians, no microphone stands with people, no audience and no club tables. Original design only. Fresh composition from this text only; no input or reference images.
