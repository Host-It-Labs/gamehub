Use case: ui-mockup. Create one complete in-game screen of a desktop browser card game, concept art for a new art direction of a trick-taking game called Nox, played at night in a harbour. Landscape 1536x1024 PNG, largest native landscape size, highest quality, no watermark.

Style, direction SIGNAL: confident mid-century modernist graphic design, like a 1960s Swiss or Scandinavian travel poster crossed with the international maritime signal flags. Everything is built from a small number of large, clean, flat shapes with crisp edges: rectangles, circles, half circles, triangles and long parallel stripes, arranged on a clear grid with generous calm areas. Flat colour only: no brush texture, no paper grain, no ink texture, no painterly shading, no fine detail. Depth comes only from overlapping flat shapes, flat offset shadows and one translucent beam of light. Palette, strictly limited: deep midnight navy and a darker blue-black for the night, a muted petrol teal for the water, warm cream, and the signal-flag colours signal red, signal yellow and a clear flag blue, used sparingly as accents. It looks designed, deliberate, witty and calm, like a real printed poster by a great graphic designer, never like a digital painting.

World: a nocturnal harbour of improvised boats and pirate radio, seen straight from above like a flat harbour plan. The play surface is the flat hatch cover of a moored work barge: one large rectangle of deep navy painted steel, face-on and perfectly flat, with a slim cream painted line running around it a little inside its edge, and outside that line a calm band of darker navy steel on all four sides. This rim band is empty and quiet so that name tags can sit on it anywhere. Around the barge, filling the picture to every edge: blue-black water drawn as long horizontal stripes of slightly different blues, broken by short cream and yellow dashes for reflected harbour lights; along the top, a strip of stone quay drawn as flat grey-blue blocks with round black bollards, one coiled rope drawn as a cream spiral, and a line of small square and triangular signal-flag bunting in red, yellow, blue and cream; at the upper right, the end of a breakwater with a small round lighthouse seen from above, its single pale yellow beam a long translucent wedge that falls across the upper right of the scene and just over one corner of the barge; at the upper left, a thin radio mast seen from above as a small cross with three thin concentric cream arcs of radio waves spreading from it; to the left and to the right of the barge, two or three small moored boats seen from above as simple lozenge and teardrop shapes in cream, red and teal, each with one or two circles for hatches. Light: the lighthouse at the upper right is the single light source; every flat shadow falls toward the lower left.

Layout of the game screen, drawn in this style. The barge hatch, the play area, is a wide rectangle spanning about the middle 72 percent of the image width and running from about 13 percent to about 70 percent of the image height; the harbour surrounds it on every side. Four players sit around the table, each shown only as a compact seat tag lying on the rim band: a small circular avatar made of a simple flat geometric signal-flag pattern (no faces), a short name and a small score number. One tag lies on the bottom rim a little left of centre (the viewer), one on the middle of the left rim, one on the middle of the top rim and one on the middle of the right rim. The top rim tag has a bright signal-yellow ring around its avatar, showing that it is that player's turn. Just inside the cream line, four small flat cream chevron arrows, one on each side, point clockwise around the table. In the middle of the hatch lies the current trick: three face-up cards lying flat and straight, slightly apart, each one nudged toward the player who played it (left, top and right), each with a flat offset shadow toward the lower left. No chairs, no seats, no people and no hands anywhere.

Cards: crisp cream-white cards with small rounded corners and a thin inner border, each with one large bold numeral at the top left in a heavy modernist sans serif and one large flat geometric suit shape in the centre. Five suits, each with its own signal colour: spades black, hearts signal red, clubs flag blue, diamonds signal yellow, and a fifth suit, Storm, whose card face is split diagonally into signal yellow and black with a bold cream lightning bolt; the storm cards are the dangerous ones.

The hand dock at the bottom, below the barge, over the water: a long floating tray with rounded ends in flat navy with a thin cream edge, about 60 percent of the image width, resting just above the bottom edge, holding ten overlapping face-up cards in one straight row, each overlapping the next so that its numeral and suit stay visible; one card is lifted clear above the others and brought to the front, selected. At the left end of the tray: one flat die drawn as a cream rounded square showing a red heart suit shape, and beside it two small round flat tokens, a blue circle with a cream shield shape and the number 2, and a red circle with a cream grappling-hook shape. At the right end of the tray: a flat signal-red rounded rectangular button with the single word Play in cream, and next to it a small round cream button with a curved undo arrow.

Navigation drawn on top, and nothing else: top-left corner, a small round cream button with a left arrow, and beside it the word Nox in a bold modernist sans serif. Top-right corner, one small round cream menu button with three short horizontal lines. Top centre, one small cream pill showing 2/4. No other interface, no status bar, no captions, no helper text.

Text: the only words in the image are Nox and Play. The only other characters are the card numerals from 1 to 10, the round count 2/4, the small score numbers and the four short names on the seat tags: You, Rook, Static and Moth. No other letters, words, labels, logos, brand names or watermark anywhere, not on the boats, the quay or the flags.

Not like: not a painted or rendered fantasy pirate scene; no treasure chest, no gold coins, no skulls, no tricorn hats, no parrots, no jolly roger, no candles, no rats, no dramatic glow and no clutter. Not cut paper or kraft card. Not woodcut, linocut, risograph or any ink printmaking texture. Not cel-shaded outlined cartoon or anime. Not a 3D render. Not a stock vector illustration with gradients. Original design only. Fresh composition from this text only; no input or reference images.
