Use case: ui-mockup. Create one complete in-game screen of a desktop browser card game, concept art for a new art direction of a trick-taking game called Nox, played at night in a harbour. Landscape 1536x1024 PNG, largest native landscape size, highest quality, no watermark.

Style, direction LANTERN: a real night-time flat-lay photograph, shot straight down with a medium-format camera, of a card game laid out in the cockpit of a small old wooden fishing boat moored in a harbour at night. Everything is a real, tactile, well-worn object: varnished teak with warm honey grain and fine dark seams, tarred rope, scuffed brass, cream bakelite, painted wood with chipped enamel, a few rain beads on the varnish. One warm paraffin storm lantern stands on the deck just beyond the far edge of the table; only its brass base and its round warm glow show at the top of the picture. Its light falls from the top of the picture toward the viewer: warm amber on the teak, long soft shadows falling downward, and the rest of the scene sinking into deep blue night shadow toward the edges. Colour: warm amber and honey teak against cold deep navy night, with small accents of oxblood red and bottle green. Quiet, intimate, cinematic and restrained, like an editorial still-life photograph for a high-end magazine: few objects, each one real and specific, large calm areas, natural fine film grain. It must look like an actual photograph of real things, never like a digital painting or a render.

World: the improvised boats and pirate radio of a nocturnal harbour, felt through the owner's belongings. The play surface is a rectangular varnished teak cockpit table seen perfectly face-on from above, completely flat, its planks running left to right, framed on all four sides by a raised teak fiddle rail about two fingers wide. That rail is a calm, continuous rim band, empty so that name tags can lie on it anywhere. Around the table, filling the picture to every edge: the dark slatted teak floor grating of the cockpit; at the upper left, a small battered transistor radio in cream bakelite with a round speaker grille and a softly glowing amber tuning dial, its telescopic aerial extended diagonally; at the upper right, a neatly coiled tarred rope beside a brass cleat; at the lower left corner, a folded paper sea chart, partly open, its printed lines too small to read; at the far right, a strip of the white-painted gunwale of the boat, and beyond it a band of black harbour water with long wavering reflections of distant orange and white harbour lights; at the lower right corner, an old brass hand-bearing compass. Light: the lantern above the far edge is the single light source; every shadow falls downward, toward the viewer.

Layout of the game screen, in this photograph. The teak table, the play area, is a wide rectangle spanning about the middle 72 percent of the image width and running from about 13 percent to about 70 percent of the image height; the boat surrounds it on every side. Four players sit around the table, each shown only as a compact seat tag lying on the fiddle rail: a small rounded plate of cream enamel with a thin brass edge, holding a small round enamel avatar disc with a simple two-colour pattern (no faces), a short stamped name and a small score number. One tag lies on the bottom rail a little left of centre (the viewer), one on the middle of the left rail, one on the middle of the top rail and one on the middle of the right rail. The top rail tag has a warm glowing amber ring around its avatar, showing that it is that player's turn. Set into the inner edge of the rail, four small brass arrow inlays, one on each side, point clockwise around the table. In the middle of the table lies the current trick: three face-up cards lying flat, slightly apart and slightly askew, each one nudged toward the player who played it (left, top and right), each casting a soft shadow downward. No chairs, no cushions, no seats, no people and no hands anywhere.

Cards: real printed playing cards on thick cream stock with a soft linen finish and rounded corners, each with a bold classic serif numeral at the top left and one large suit symbol in the centre, printed in deep inks. Five suits: spades black, hearts oxblood red, clubs bottle green, diamonds deep ultramarine, and a fifth suit, Storm, printed on a charcoal card with a silver foil lightning bolt and a silver numeral; the storm cards are the dangerous ones.

The hand at the bottom, below the table: a long, narrow varnished teak tray with a brass lip lies across the bottom of the picture, about 60 percent of the image width, just above the bottom edge, holding ten face-up cards in one straight overlapping row, each overlapping the next so that its numeral and suit stay visible; one card is lifted and slid up out of the row, selected, casting a larger soft shadow. At the left end of the tray: one real ivory bone die in a small round brass dish, its top face carved with a red heart suit symbol, and beside it two small tokens: a round brass shield token stamped with the number 2, and a small iron grappling-hook charm on a short cord. At the right end of the tray: a rectangular polished brass push plate engraved with the single word Play, and next to it a small round brass button engraved with a curved undo arrow.

Navigation, the only non-photographic elements, drawn as small clean translucent dark overlays with cream marks, and nothing else: top-left corner, a small round button with a left arrow, and beside it the word Nox in a refined serif. Top-right corner, one small round menu button with three short horizontal lines. Top centre, one small dark pill showing 2/4. No other interface, no status bar, no captions, no helper text.

Text: the only words in the image are Nox and Play. The only other characters are the card numerals from 1 to 10, the round count 2/4, the small score numbers and the four short names on the seat tags: You, Rook, Static and Moth. No other letters, words, labels, logos, brand names or watermark anywhere, not on the radio, the chart, the compass or the boat.

Not like: not a painted or rendered fantasy pirate scene; no treasure chest, no gold coins, no skulls, no tricorn hats, no parrots, no jolly roger, no candles, no rats and no clutter. Not cut paper or kraft card. Not woodcut, linocut, risograph or any ink printmaking. Not cel-shaded outlined cartoon or anime. Not a 3D render, not tilt-shift toy photography, no miniatures and no plastic toys. Not a stock photo. Original design only. Fresh composition from this text only; no input or reference images.
