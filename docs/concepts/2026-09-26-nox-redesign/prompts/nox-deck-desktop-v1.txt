Use case: ui-mockup. Create one complete in-game screen of a desktop browser card game, concept art for a new art direction of a pirate-themed trick-taking game called Nox, played at night. Landscape 1536x1024 PNG, largest native landscape size, highest quality, no watermark.

Style, direction DECK: confident mid-century modernist flat graphic design, like a bold 1950s swashbuckler film poster reduced to its strongest shapes. Everything is built from a small number of large, clean, flat shapes with crisp edges: rectangles, circles, half circles, triangles, crescents and long parallel stripes, arranged on a clear grid with generous calm areas. Flat colour only: no brush texture, no paper grain, no ink texture, no painterly shading, no soft gradients, no fine detail. Depth comes only from overlapping flat shapes, flat offset shadows and one translucent flat shape of light. The pirate motifs (skulls, crossbones, cutlasses, flags, ships, treasure) are drawn as bold, witty, simplified graphic symbols, the way a great poster designer would draw them, never as detailed illustration. It looks designed, deliberate and full of swagger yet calm, like a real printed poster by a great graphic designer, never like a digital painting. Palette, strictly limited: midnight navy and a darker blue-black, a deep sea teal, warm cream, oxblood red, and brass ochre as the accent.

World: the deck of a pirate ship at anchor on a moonlit night, seen straight from above like a flat deck plan. The play surface is the main cargo hatch in the middle of the deck: one large rectangle of dark navy planking drawn as a few long flat stripes, with a slim cream line painted around it a little inside its edge; the rim band outside that line is the thick, calm timber coaming of the hatch, continuous on all four sides. Around it, filling the picture to every edge: the rest of the deck as long flat plank stripes in navy and teal; the bases of two masts seen from above as bold circles, each crossed by a long yard with a furled cream sail drawn as a rounded bar; black cannons along the rails at both sides, drawn as simple black bars with round muzzles pointing outward through the gunwale; coiled ropes as cream spirals; a ship's wheel as a bold circle with spokes at the upper right; beyond the rails at the left and right edges, the night sea in flat stripes of blue-black with short cream dashes of moonlight; a big black pirate flag with a bold cream skull and crossbones lying spread out on the deck at the upper left, as one of the largest shapes in the picture; and one huge oxblood-red kraken tentacle with round cream suckers curling up over the rail at the lower right corner, beside the dock. Light: the full moon beyond the upper left edge is the single light source; its light is one long translucent cream wedge falling across the upper left of the deck, and every flat shadow falls toward the lower right.

Layout of the game screen, drawn in this style. The play area is a wide rectangle, face-on and perfectly flat in true plan view, spanning about the middle 72 percent of the image width and running from about 13 percent to about 70 percent of the image height; the scenery surrounds it on every side and fills the picture to every edge. Nothing stands inside the play area: it is one calm, even surface. Around the play area runs a calm, continuous rim band, empty so that name tags can sit on it anywhere. Four players sit around the table, each shown only as a compact horizontal pill-shaped seat tag lying flat on the rim band, its text upright and horizontal: a small circular avatar showing one simple flat pirate-flag emblem (a skull, crossed swords, an hourglass or a heart pierced by a sword, one per player), a short name and a small score number. The viewer's tag lies on the bottom rim, clearly left of centre, about one third of the way along from the left end; the others lie on the middle of the left rim, the middle of the top rim and the middle of the right rim. The top rim tag has a bright ring in the accent colour around its avatar, showing that it is that player's turn. Just inside the rim, four small flat chevron arrows, one at the middle of each side, chase each other clockwise around the table: the one on the top side points right, the one on the right side points down, the one on the bottom side points left and the one on the left side points up. In the middle of the play area lies the current trick: three face-up cards lying flat and straight, slightly apart, each one nudged toward the player who played it (left, top and right), each with a flat offset shadow that falls away from the light. No chairs, no stools, no benches, no people, no pirates and no hands anywhere.

Cards: crisp cream-white cards with small rounded corners and a thin inner border, each with one large bold numeral at the top left in a heavy modernist sans serif and one large flat geometric suit shape in the centre. Five suits, each with its own colour: spades black, hearts oxblood red, clubs deep sea teal, diamonds brass ochre, and a fifth suit, Storm, whose card face is split diagonally into brass ochre and blue-black with a bold cream lightning bolt. The storm cards are the dangerous ones.

The hand dock at the bottom, below the play area: a long floating tray with rounded ends in flat blue-black with a thin cream edge, like a length of ship's rail, about 60 percent of the image width, resting just above the bottom edge, holding ten overlapping face-up cards in one straight row, each overlapping the next so that its numeral and suit stay visible, one of them a storm card; one card is lifted clear above the others and brought to the front, selected. At the left end of the tray: one flat die drawn as a cream rounded square showing a heart suit shape, and beside it two small round flat tokens, one with a shield shape and the number 2, one with a grappling hook, a straight shaft ending in four curved claws with a ring at the top. At the right end of the tray: a flat rounded rectangular button in the accent colour with the single word Play, and next to it a small round cream button with a curved undo arrow.

Navigation drawn on top, and nothing else: top-left corner, a small round cream button with a left arrow, and beside it the word Nox in a bold display typeface in the spirit of the poster. Top-right corner, one small round cream menu button with three short horizontal lines. Top centre, one small cream pill showing 2/4. No other interface, no status bar, no captions, no helper text.

Text: the only words in the image are Nox and Play. The only other characters are the card numerals from 1 to 10, the round count 2/4, the small score numbers and the four short names on the seat tags: You, Rook, Static and Moth. No other letters, words, labels, logos, brand names or watermark anywhere, not on the flag, the sails, the cannons or the deck.

Not like: not a painted or rendered fantasy pirate illustration, no digital painting, no realistic lighting, no glow effects, no piles of detailed loot and no clutter. Not cut paper or kraft card. Not woodcut, linocut, risograph or any ink printmaking texture. Not cel-shaded outlined cartoon or anime. Not a 3D render. Not a stock vector illustration with gradients. No existing film, franchise or brand. Original design only. Fresh composition from this text only; no input or reference images.
